# QCD Issue Tree for Production Analysis

## When to Use This Skill

Use this skill when:
- Analyzing production site issues or operational challenges
- Evaluating new production technology or automation (e.g., introducing robots)
- Identifying bottlenecks in manufacturing processes
- Balancing competing priorities between HQ and site teams
- Investigating why production improvements aren't translating to sales

## Prerequisites

Before applying this framework, ensure you have:
- A production site or process to analyze
- A specific operational challenge or improvement initiative
- Access to production data and site personnel input
- Safety measures already established (safety is always the first priority)

## The QCD Framework

The QCD framework evaluates production decisions across three interdependent criteria:

1. **Quality (Q)**: Can quality be maintained or improved?
   - Often the primary focus of site experts
   - Directly impacts customer satisfaction and brand reputation

2. **Cost (C)**: Will costs decrease?
   - Often the primary focus of HQ experts
   - Includes direct costs and hidden costs (time spent on fixes)

3. **Delivery (D)**: Can production stabilize quickly?
   - Often the overlooked blind spot
   - Critical for meeting customer demand and cash flow

## Execution Steps

### Step 1: Establish Safety First
- Confirm all safety protocols are in place
- Safety must be ensured before any other consideration
- Do not proceed if safety is compromised

### Step 2: Evaluate the Three Core Criteria

For each production decision or challenge, assess:

**Cost Analysis:**
- Will this reduce direct production costs?
- What are the implementation costs?
- Consider both short-term and long-term cost implications

**Quality Analysis:**
- Can current quality levels be maintained?
- Will quality improve or degrade?
- What are the quality risks and mitigation strategies?

**Delivery Analysis:**
- How quickly can production stabilize?
- What is the time-to-stable-production?
- Will delivery timelines be impacted during transition?

### Step 3: Analyze Interdependence

Recognize that QCD criteria are not independent:

- Quality issues directly cause sales to drop
- Delivery delays directly cause sales to drop
- Time spent improving Quality or fixing Delivery delays IS a Cost
- Cost-cutting that harms Quality or Delivery creates hidden costs

### Step 4: Focus on the Blind Spot (Delivery Speed)

Identify which perspective is dominating the analysis:

**HQ Expert Bias:**
- Typically focuses primarily on Cost
- May underestimate time-to-stability
- May overlook production disruption risks

**Site Expert Bias:**
- Typically focuses on Quality and Delivery
- May resist necessary cost optimizations
- May have local optimization blind spots

**Strategic Insight:**
- Delivery Speed (time to stabilize production) is often the critical bottleneck
- Example: Tesla's robot production struggles were fundamentally a Delivery Speed issue
- Stabilization time determines when ROI actually begins

### Step 5: Break Down Sub-Issues

For Delivery Speed analysis, decompose into two series:

**Human Series (Skills Acquisition):**
- How long does it take workers to master new processes?
- What training is required?
- What is the learning curve duration?

**Tool Series (Stabilization Frameworks):**
- What frameworks exist to accelerate stabilization?
- Are there standardized procedures?
- What tools or automation can reduce variability?

## Output

This analysis produces:
1. Identification of the critical bottleneck (often Delivery Speed)
2. Understanding of trade-offs between Q, C, and D
3. A plan to address Human vs. Tool constraints
4. Strategic focus on the most impactful improvement area

## Key Insight

The essence of many production problems is not Cost or Quality, but **Delivery Speed**—the time required to stabilize new production processes. This is the blind spot that often determines project success or failure.
