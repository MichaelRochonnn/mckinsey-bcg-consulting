# Team Facilitation and Collaborative Problem-Solving

## When to Use
- Facing complex problems requiring team-based solutions
- Need to generate rapid idea generation from groups
- Managing tense meetings with potential conflict or resistance
- Determining appropriate team size and structure for projects

## Core Workflow

### 1. Form Appropriate Teams
Never work alone on complex problems:

- **Minimum Team**: 2 people (e.g., consultant and project manager) for limited scope
- **Standard Team**: 5-6 people for typical problem-solving
- **Super-Team**: Multiple 5-6 person teams for maximum clients (e.g., 20+ person projects requiring hotel conference rooms)

**Team Value Logic**:
- Complexity requires diverse perspectives beyond individual capability
- More people enable greater data collection and analysis capacity
- Multiple minds generate richer interpretation of data meaning

### 2. Facilitate Structured Brainstorming
Use Post-it Exercise for rapid idea generation:

- **Preparation**: Provide each participant with a stack of sticky notes
- **Generation**: Have participants independently write all ideas, one idea per note
- **Collection**: Gather notes from all participants
- **Presentation**: Leader reads aloud each idea to the group
- **Constraint**: Do not discuss each idea immediately; prioritize speed and volume over immediate evaluation

### 3. Manage Tense Meetings
Apply Preemptive Control Technique when facing resistance:

- **Setup**: Gather all relevant stakeholders in large conference room
- **Frame**: Present the change issue or plan under consideration
- **Control**: Immediately ask participants to voice dissatisfaction with the proposed plan
- **Purpose**: By actively soliciting negative feedback first, you establish meeting control and surface objections in a managed way
- **Application**: Particularly effective for change management discussions in tense atmospheres

## Key Principles
- **Collaboration Over Individualism**: Complex problems require team solutions; solo work cannot meet high standards for difficult issues
- **Psychological Safety**: Create environments where unconventional ideas and negative feedback can surface without punishment
- **Structured Creativity**: Use physical artifacts (sticky notes) and clear protocols to manage group dynamics

## Output
Appropriately staffed teams, generated idea inventories, and controlled meeting environments that surface critical feedback without derailing objectives.
