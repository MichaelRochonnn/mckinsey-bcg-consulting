# Expert Interviewing and Information Extraction

## When to Use
- Need to gather specific information from subject matter experts or stakeholders
- Facing interviewees who claim ignorance or lack of ideas
- Conducting interviews in sensitive or tense environments
- Following up after significant time investments by interviewees

## Core Workflow

### 1. Apply Seven Core Interview Strategies

**Leverage Authority**: Have the interviewee's boss arrange the meeting and communicate importance. When interviewees know their superiors support the interview, misleading or敷衍 responses decrease significantly.

**Use Two-Person Teams**: Conduct interviews in pairs:
- One person asks questions while the other takes notes
- Prevents missing non-verbal cues due to note-taking distraction
- Provides dual perspectives and specialized expertise
- Ensure questioner and note-taker maintain synchronization

**Listen, Don't Lead**: 
- Avoid seeking simple yes/no answers
- Obtain open-ended, detailed responses through active listening
- Speak minimally; ensure only that the interview stays on topic
- Remember: Interviewees usually know their industry better than you

**Paraphrase Extensively**: 
- Restate interviewee answers in organized form
- Confirms correct understanding
- Provides opportunity for interviewees to add information or emphasize key points

**Use Indirect Approaches**: 
- Avoid interrogation-style questioning
- Excessive pressure triggers defensive mechanisms and potential refusal to cooperate
- Maintain conversational tone rather than aggressive questioning

**Limit Question Quantity**:
- Focus on 2-3 critical questions; avoid wasting time on known information
- Recognize that business interviews are often unpleasant experiences
- Stop before reaching hostility or non-cooperation; leave room for future contact

**Apply the Columbo Strategy**:
- **Basic**: When leaving, pretend to remember one last question and turn back—often yields crucial information
- **Advanced (Super Columbo)**: Wait 1-2 days, then return casually asking a follow-up question—reduces aggression and increases information flow

### 2. Push Past "I Have No Ideas"
When interviewees respond with "I have no ideas" or "I don't know":

- **Interpret the Excuse**: Recognize this usually means "I'm too busy," "I'm not smart enough," or "I'm too lazy"
- **Treat as Challenge**: View this as a starting point, not an endpoint—like a sculptor removing everything that doesn't look like an elephant
- **Ask Specific Questions**: Drill down with targeted questions (e.g., "Do you know competitor advertising spending?")
- **Accept Educated Guesses**: 
  - Numbers need not be 100% accurate
  - Estimates within 2x of actual values are acceptable for business decisions
  - Precision sufficient for decision-making is the standard
- **Maintain Standards**: Do not accept "I have no ideas" from yourself or others; with thought and investigation, useful information always exists

### 3. Execute Post-Interview Protocol
For interviews exceeding 30 minutes:

- **Timing**: Return to office and write immediately
- **Format**: Use company letterhead with carefully crafted, personalized language
- **Content**: 
  - Express appreciation for their time
  - Demonstrate professionalism
  - Avoid generic templates; customize to avoid computer-generated appearance
- **Value**: Leaves positive impression and builds long-term relationships; significant returns possible (e.g., clients displaying letters for years)

## Key Principles
- **Persistence Over Acceptance**: Do not accept surface-level ignorance; dig deeper with specific questions
- **Relationship Management**: Maintain goodwill for future information needs
- **Precision Standards**: Accept directional accuracy over perfect precision for business decisions

## Output
Detailed information sufficient for business decision-making, maintained professional relationships, and documented insights for analysis.
