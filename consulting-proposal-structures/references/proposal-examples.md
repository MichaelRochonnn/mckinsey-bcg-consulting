# Proposal Structure Examples

## McKinsey Style Example

**Situation**: A retail company with declining revenue

**Presentation**:
1. "Your company must close 30% of underperforming stores within 6 months to survive."
2. "This conclusion is based on three reasons: declining foot traffic, rising operational costs, and e-commerce competition."
3. "We will implement this by: [3 action steps]."
4. "Why hasn't this been done yet? Management has been attached to legacy locations and fears PR backlash."
5. "We address this by: [mitigation strategy]."

## Boston Style Example

**Situation**: Same retail company with declining revenue

**Presentation**:
1. "What patterns do you see in your store performance data over the last 3 years?"
2. [Show data] "Notice how 30% of stores contribute to -15% of profit while using 40% of resources."
3. "What would happen if we could redirect those resources?"
4. "What patterns emerge among these underperforming locations?"
5. [Audience realizes they need to close stores]
6. "Exactly - so what's the logical next step?"

## Hybrid Approach Example

**Situation**: Mixed audience - some executives impatient, some middle managers resistant

**Presentation**:
1. "We have a critical decision to make about our store portfolio."
2. "Before I share our recommendation, let's look at the data together."
3. [Guided discovery with key executives getting to conclusion]
4. "Based on what we've seen, the conclusion is clear: close 30% of stores."
5. "Here's the action plan to make this happen."

## Common Pitfalls

### McKinsey Style Pitfalls
- Using with highly resistant audiences causes shutdown
- Overwhelming audience with conclusion without enough evidence
- Failing to adequately address the suffocation point
- Not adapting to cultural preference for indirectness

### Boston Style Pitfalls
- Taking too long when time is constrained
- Losing audience in too many questions
- Failing to reach clear conclusion
- Audience doesn't actually discover the answer

## Cultural Adaptations

**US/Western Europe**: Generally more receptive to McKinsey style
**Japan/Asia**: Often prefer Boston or hybrid approaches
**Germany**: Prefers thorough evidence in either style
**Startups**: Often appreciate McKinsey directness
**Legacy organizations**: May require Boston style for buy-in
