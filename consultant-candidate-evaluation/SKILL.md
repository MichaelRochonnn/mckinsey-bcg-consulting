# Consultant Candidate Evaluation

Use this skill to systematically evaluate consulting candidates against three critical dimensions that predict success in consulting roles.

## When to Use

- Recruiting for consulting firms or internal consulting roles
- Screening candidates for consulting positions
- Assessing whether a job seeker has the right profile for consulting
- Evaluating internal candidates for promotion to consulting roles

## Evaluation Framework

Assess candidates across three distinct criteria:

### 1. Left Brain (Logical Thinking)

**What to assess:**
- Ability to think logically and analytically
- Problem-solving through structured reasoning

**Assessment approach:**
- This skill can be improved through practice
- Most consulting aspirants possess this or can pass exams through training
- Use case interviews, logic puzzles, or analytical tests

### 2. Right Brain (Pattern Recognition)

**What to assess:**
- Ability to grasp concepts via images and mental models
- Intuition and creativity in problem-solving
- Capacity to match current problems against past experienced models

**Assessment approach:**
- Very difficult to train—requires approximately 3,000 problem-solving models stored in the brain
- Equivalent to 25 years of experience if untrained
- Look for candidates who demonstrate intuitive leaps and creative problem framing
- Similar to AI deep learning: pattern matching against stored models

### 3. First Experience (Curiosity)

**What to assess:**
- Deep curiosity ("What? Why? How?")
- Objective introspection about oneself
- Strength of "inner self" and breadth of perspective

**Assessment approach:**
- Look for specific "First Experiences" that forced self-redefinition:
  - Long-term overseas living
  - NPO/volunteer activities involving hardship
  - Deep introspection during youth (e.g., social withdrawal periods)
- Ask about transformative life experiences
- Evaluate depth of self-awareness and perspective

## Red Flags (Exclusion Criteria)

### Consultant Mutants
- Candidates who are only abnormally good at logical thinking
- Those who see one pattern as absolute truth
- Lack creativity and flexibility in thinking

### Law Graduates (General Rule)
- Typically trained to maintain order and rules
- Consulting requires breaking and rethinking rules

**Exceptions:**
- Law graduates with bad grades in law
- Law graduates with good grades in philosophy or other non-legal subjects

## Decision Framework

Make a Hire/No Hire decision based on the balance of:
1. **Logic** (Left Brain) - baseline requirement, trainable
2. **Creativity/Models** (Right Brain) - critical differentiator, hard to train
3. **Depth of Character** (First Experience) - indicates long-term potential

**Ideal candidate:** Strong across all three dimensions, with particular emphasis on Right Brain and First Experience as these are difficult to develop.
