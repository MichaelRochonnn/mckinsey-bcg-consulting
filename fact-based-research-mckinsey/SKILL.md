# Fact-Based Research and Solution Development

## When to Use
- Tempted to apply past solutions to new problems without validation
- Starting data collection for hypothesis validation
- Need to balance efficiency (leveraging existing work) with rigor (fact-based validation)
- Risk of "hammer and nail" cognitive bias (using familiar tools for all problems)

## Core Workflow

### 1. Avoid Cookie-Cutter Solutions
Resist the temptation to apply standard answers:

- **Recognize the Trap**: Avoid "if you only have a hammer, every problem looks like a nail" syndrome
- **Trust But Verify**: Accept that intuition is often correct, but always verify with facts before concluding
- **Execute Rigorous Analysis**:
  - Apply specific analytical tools (demand curve analysis, break-even calculations, budget analysis)
  - Complete thorough evidence gathering before making recommendations
  - Recognize that tools remain consistent, but their application must be customized
- **Handle Counter-Intuitive Results**: Be prepared for facts to contradict intuition (e.g., while 80% of pricing studies suggest raising prices, fact-based analysis may reveal you should lower them)
- **Ensure Acceptance**: Fact-based conclusions are more likely to be accepted by stakeholders than intuitive judgments

### 2. Leverage Existing Resources
Never reinvent the wheel; assume someone has studied similar problems:

**Access Internal Databases**:
- Search company databases (e.g., McKinsey's PD network) for similar industries and problems
- Review recent projects and internal research reports
- Invest time filtering through large result sets to find relevant files
- Use findings to guide project direction effectively

**Consult Expert Resources**:
- Engage information specialists who can access multiple sources (databases, libraries)
- Consult subject matter experts in specific domains (banking, manufacturing, etc.)
- Contact colleagues who have solved similar problems
- Reach out to professionals at other companies who have addressed comparable issues

**Utilize External Sources**:
- Access commercial databases (Lexis/Nexis, Dun & Bradstreet, Datastream)
- Review industry publications, business journals, and books
- Use internet resources and commercial libraries

**Adapt for Environment**:
- In large companies: Use corporate knowledge bases, training manuals, and experienced colleagues
- Working independently: Rely on industry magazines, data packages, internet, and local libraries

## Key Principles
- **Efficiency Through Leverage**: Time is valuable; build on existing work rather than starting from scratch
- **Validation Required**: Past solutions provide starting points, not final answers
- **Resourcefulness**: Combine internal knowledge with external data for comprehensive analysis

## Output
A customized, fact-validated solution supported by existing research and new data analysis, avoiding template-based recommendations.
