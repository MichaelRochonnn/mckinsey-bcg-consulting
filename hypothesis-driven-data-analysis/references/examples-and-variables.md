# Examples and Variables

## Real-World Examples

### Example 1: Diapers and Beer Correlation

**Data Mining Finding**: Big Data analysis revealed a correlation between diaper and beer purchases.

**Risk**: Without a logical hypothesis, this correlation might be coincidental or context-specific.

**Action Required**: Verify the "why" - is there a logical customer behavior explanation, or is this a statistical anomaly?

---

### Example 2: Aristotle and Accounting Books

**Data Mining Finding**: Customers buying Aristotle books also tend to buy accounting books.

**Risk**: This correlation might be due to a specific training class curriculum rather than genuine customer preference.

**Action Required**: Investigate whether the correlation is driven by a temporary factor (class requirement) or a lasting customer need.

---

### Example 3: Seiyu Supermarket Baby Corner (Success Story)

**Hypothesis**: A merchandiser hypothesized that mothers need baby items in one place due to the "15-minute" shopping window before a baby cries.

**Customer Scenario**: Mother enters store with baby → has limited time → needs all baby-related items quickly → baby becomes fussy after ~15 minutes

**Action**: Created a "Baby Corner" consolidating all baby-related products

**Result**: Despite contradicting standard "Category Management" practices, the hypothesis-driven approach succeeded because it was based on deep customer empathy and logical causation.

---

## Key Variables

### Correlation
- **Type**: Statistical measure
- **Definition**: A relationship between two variables without implying cause
- **Example**: When variable A increases, variable B also increases
- **Limitation**: Does not explain WHY the relationship exists

### Hypothesis
- **Type**: Proposition
- **Definition**: A proposed explanation or prediction to be tested
- **Characteristics**: Should be simple, logical, and grounded in customer understanding
- **Example**: "Mothers need baby items consolidated because they have limited shopping time"
