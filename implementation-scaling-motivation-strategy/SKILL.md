# Implementation Scaling and Motivation Strategy

This skill guides the execution of strategic solutions by structuring the scaling process and aligning organizational motivation. Understanding the solution is insufficient; sustained motivation and proper scaling are critical for long-term success.

## Prerequisites
- Approved proposal or strategy document
- Initial stakeholder buy-in
- Defined strategic solution

## Trigger Condition
Execute this workflow **IF** a strategic solution has been proposed and requires organizational scaling.

## Execution Workflow

### 1. Determine the Scaling Stage
Assess the current state of the solution to identify the appropriate phase in the **0-1-10-100 Framework**:

- **0 to 1 (Discovery):** Finding the answer from nothing. This is the most creative step.
- **1 to 10 (Initial Action):** Concrete start, securing early wins, and horizontal expansion. Critical for building momentum.
- **10 to 100 (Organizational Change):** Expanding the proven success to the entire organization.
- **100+ (Embedding):** Institutionalizing the cycle of evolution into the organization's DNA.

### 2. Select a Motivation Driver
Choose the primary driver to sustain organizational energy based on the context and duration of the change:

- **Achievement:** Use for short-term goals. Driven by the mindset "Because it is there." Requires clear, specific goals.
- **Crisis:** Use for urgent turnarounds (McKinsey approach). Creates urgency to force change (the "boiling frog" effect). Effective short-term (e.g., 3 months) but carries a high risk of relapse to comfort once the crisis subsides.
- **Mission:** Use for long-term transformation (Boston approach). Creates a shared purpose driven by "Why do we exist?" This is the most sustainable and persistent driver.

### 3. Apply Implementation Practices
Execute the following steps to bridge the gap between the goal and reality:

1. **Share a Common Goal:** Ensure alignment across the organization on the primary objective.
2. **Clarify the Gap:** Explicitly state the difference between the current reality and the desired goal.
3. **Dig into the Root Cause:** Analyze why the gap was left unaddressed in the past.
4. **Define Actions:** Create a concrete action plan specifying the timeline and the responsible person for each task.

## Output
The result of this skill is a roadmap detailing the scaling phases and a strategy for maintaining organizational momentum using the selected motivation driver.
